# Challenges of Building the Gym

Building a gym environment for industrial maintenance follows no settled formalism, so the hydraulic rig gym had to define what an episode is, how time and cost enter it, what a repair does to the asset, and how an episode is scored without a judge. The table lists the roadblocks met while building the gym, what went wrong or was missing, the solution in this gym, the place in the code, and the test or ablation that checks the solution. One paragraph per row below the table gives the reasoning behind the row. The page states no value from a run.

The related pages are [README.md](README.md) for the index, [environment.md](environment.md) for the episode rules and the verifier, [environment-actions-mcp.md](environment-actions-mcp.md) and [causal-diagnosis-mcp.md](causal-diagnosis-mcp.md) for the two servers, [experiments.md](experiments.md) for the drivers and the ablations, and [adding-a-dataset.md](adding-a-dataset.md) for what a new asset class needs. The ablations carry the identifiers of `CausalDiagnosis/experiments/table_definitions/experiment_tables.py`, Sc for scoring, En for the environment, Ag for the agent, and L for the options of a layer, and `CausalDiagnosis/evaluation/result_tables/tables_from_runs/` builds their tables.

## The challenges in detail

### An episode without a formal definition

Gymnasium fixes the interface of an environment, reset and step, and leaves the content of an episode to its author, while AssetOpsBench scores the final answer to a question with an LLM judge, and none of its actions changes the condition of an asset. The hydraulic rig gym writes the content down as a contract. `EpisodeConfig` holds every parameter besides the seed, from the sensor sets and the prices to the hidden processes and the clock mode, and `validate` refuses a bad table with every problem listed at once. `reset(seed)` draws the hidden combination, the served window and the schedule from one seeded generator in this order, `step` returns the answer envelope with the cost the step charged, and an episode ends for one of five reasons, `finish`, `budget`, `horizon`, `max_steps` or `failure`, which `_end` writes into the end entry of the log. As a result, a seed, a configuration, and an action sequence reproduce the log byte for byte, and the action server and the verifier both build on this replay.

### Time that passes while the agent thinks

A degrading asset accrues downtime while an agent deliberates, and in Gaia2, arXiv 2602.11964, removing the generation latency improves every model on the Time split, with GPT-5 at high reasoning effort moving from 0.0 to 34.4 percent, so a realistic clock charges thinking time. A clock read from the wall, however, makes an episode depend on the serving engine and breaks replay. The gym separates the two concerns with four clock modes in `CausalDiagnosis/environment/Causal_Diagnosis_Environment/clock/episode_clock.py`. The default mode, `declared`, advances the clock by the declared duration of each action, so an episode is a function of its seed, its configuration, and its actions. The `generation_time` mode adds the time since the previous call, its seconds added up from call to call and charged one cycle for every full `think_seconds_per_cycle` with the rest carried, which the servers store in the state file and pass again on replay. The `generated_tokens` mode charges the tokens that the agent's model generated since the previous call at the fixed rate `seconds_per_token` into the same carried seconds, so the cycles of an episode follow the text its model generates and not the speed or the load of the serving engine, at the price of leaving out the time to read the prompt and the delays of serving. The `instant` mode charges one cycle per step, as the instant mode of ARE does, except that `get_current_time` never moves the clock. The layer options L2 and L3 switch the thinking time and the declared action durations off, and every result table names its mode.

### Consequences without a physical plant

A gym for maintenance needs actions with consequences, and the consequence of a repair has to come from the asset rather than from a model of the asset. A simulator supplies its own ground truth, as TRACE does with a simulated intervention that provides both the label and the reward, so a score in a simulator can be no better than the simulator. The hydraulic test rig dataset of the UCI Machine Learning Repository records cycles under every combination of the condition levels of a cooler, a valve, the main pump, and the accumulator, which gives 144 combinations. A work order executes when the technician of its visit reaches it, stops the rig for its repair downtime, and closes by setting the component to its healthy level when every component it depends on is healthy, and `RigClock.set_level` then opens a window on the stable block of the repaired combination. Scheduled transitions to a worse level and those of the stochastic hazard switch the block in the same way, so every reading after a change is a recording of the changed rig. Only replacement to the healthy level can be replayed, and a repair that must halt a degradation in progress needs recordings that this asset does not hold.

### Scoring without a judge

Answer scoring cannot separate a correct diagnosis from a repair that restores the asset, and a judge that reads free text can be persuaded by it. In the ARE paper, arXiv 2509.17158, a program check of the writes reached a far higher precision against hand labels than an LLM judge reading the same trajectories, and the ARE verifier was exploited under reinforcement learning through its check of free text. As a result, the gym scores writes only. Every tool is declared read or write in the tool contract by the criterion of ARE, which asks whether the state changes, and `verify` checks the four writes against the oracle of the episode and the released evidence through the count gate, the hard checks, the timing check, the causality check, the outcome in the hidden state after the work orders, and the provenance check. No verdict of the verifier reads the closing summary, and in the failure taxonomy only the contradiction heuristic reads it. The agents table and the training table print beside the scores of the judge Cohen's kappa of its majority pass against the verified pass.

### Hidden state and the information barrier

A diagnosis task is only as difficult as its information barrier is tight, since one hidden field in an answer gives the combination away. The environment retains the full log for the verifier and serves agents views from which `public_event` strips `HIDDEN_FIELDS` at every depth. In these views, a scheduled transition to a worse level, one of the stochastic hazard, an ineffective repair, and the expiry of an emergency repair do not appear, a failure carries its time without its component, and recovery does not end the episode, since an end on recovery would reveal the hidden state. The action server writes everything an agent has been told to `released.json` after every call, and this file is the only view of the episode that the CausalDiagnosis MCP and the provenance check read. An agent cites evidence by identifiers that the episode issued, such as `obs/<n>/<field>` for a served value and `pred/<n>` for a stored prediction, and a cited identifier without a match is a failure class of its own that scores the episode as failed, with the pass term at zero and the cost and delay terms of the reward kept. The state file `episode.json` holds the seed, so an agent with file or shell tools has to run with the episode folders out of its reach, and for the same reason the driver registers only the launchers of an episode and none of the default AssetOpsBench servers, since the utilities server among them opens any JSON file.

### Tool servers without memory

The released Plan-Execute runner starts a new server process for every tool listing and every tool call, as `_list_tools` and `_call_tool` of `src/agent/plan_execute/executor.py` show, so an episode held in the memory of a server process would end after one call. As a result, the action server stores the episode in `episode.json`, and every call loads this file, builds the environment from the data file, calls `reset(seed)`, replays the stored actions with their stored thinking cycles, applies the new action, writes the files back, and answers. Replay is exact because the environment draws every random number from the generator it seeds at reset, and the CausalDiagnosis MCP follows the same rule by replaying the released observations into a fresh posterior on every call. The overhead of this design is one process start and one replay per call.

### Leakage through the recordings and the fitted models

Leakage can come from the data and the models as well as from the answers. Served in full, a stable block would reveal its combination through its length, since a reading wraps when the block ends. As a result, the environment serves the first `BLOCK_CAP` stable recordings of each combination, which is ten, cut at one starting offset per episode into two segments of five. A combination met for the first time opens a window on its live segment while a combination the rig returns to resumes its window where the rig left it. A fit that read the block an episode serves would recognize its readings, and a fit that read every block but that of the scored candidate root cause would favor the wrong candidate root causes, whose fits read the served block. The CausalDiagnosis MCP scores every candidate root cause by a fit without the block of the initial combination and without its own, so no fit reads the served block and every candidate root cause is predicted alike, and its launcher names the initial combination for that alone. Every candidate root cause also carries its uncertainty by one rule, so the spread of the resampled means of the true combination does not single it out.

### Starts that the baseline policy does not separate within the budget

Faults with similar symptoms arise wherever a policy reading the affordable channels does not separate two combinations, and the valve is an example, since its condition is read from the pressure sensors during switching, which only the full sensor set carries. A gym that demands a diagnosis on such an initial state rewards a lucky guess and penalizes a correct refusal, and training in TRACE, whose simulated intervention supplies both the label and the reward, lowers accuracy on episodes without an injected signal. `declare_insufficient_evidence` is a scored write that names the discriminating test, and the verifier counts a declaration that cites a reading released before it as a success on the initial combinations that the diagnosability label and the E18 grade of the CausalDiagnosis MCP both mark not diagnosable within the budget, and a correct diagnosis with a recovered rig as a success on every initial combination. The reward gives the declaration half the outcome of the repair, so a repair stays above a declaration after reading and that above a wrong answer, and a baseline policy that always declares shows the most a constant declaration can earn. The label is the majority of ten rollouts of the greedy baseline policy in `CausalDiagnosis/environment/reference_policies/diagnosability_label.py`. Every rollout first reads the five recordings of the initial combination before cycle 0 that the servers of AssetOpsBench return, every channel and without charge, and then buys its own readings, on ten seeds of the initial combination that lie after the eight of its pool of evaluation seeds, so that no rollout replays an episode of the test set or the episode of a reserved seed. The greedy baseline policy shares no model with the CausalDiagnosis MCP, so the label says what that policy did with the recordings that an episode serves and within the budget, and not that the data cannot tell the combination. The CausalDiagnosis MCP computes the diagnosability grade E18 of every initial combination by rolling out its decision rule on the live segment of a drawn starting offset of the true combination, and the grade is the second label under the same majority rule, more than half of the rollouts commit first to the true combination at every level. The rule runs on the fits that score the readings, and such a fit has read other recordings of the same combination, so part of the reach of the CausalDiagnosis MCP comes from those recordings. The grade and the decision of the CausalDiagnosis MCP to read first use one rule, and ablation En5 compares this rule with a fixed confidence cut.

### Cost realism

Cost turns diagnosis into a decision, since a reading, an unnecessary repair, and a missed fault have to be paid for in one unit. The recordings hold no prices, so the configuration declares a read price per cycle for each sensor set, a part price for each component, one repair charge, one visit cost per visit of the technician, one misdiagnosis price that a wrong diagnosis adds to the cost of the episode, a lead time and a repair downtime per component, and a production loss rate per component level with a higher rate while a repair stops the rig. A read price may be zero, as on the free base row, where the step cap bounds the reads and the cost of an episode counts calls. The values are design parameters of the asset file. The template `CausalDiagnosis/configs/prices/sme.json` of a further price table holds every field empty, and the driver refuses it while a field is empty. Ablation En6 repeats every cost conclusion under the default table and under a table that prices the full sensor set far above the rest. The grade table of the CausalDiagnosis MCP is keyed by a hash of these tables with the sensor sets, the horizon, and the budget, so a new table computes its own grades.

### Harness pitfalls of a small model

The released agents of AssetOpsBench expect structured tool calls and clean JSON arguments, and a small open model served locally breaks both expectations with format errors that look like reasoning errors. The chat template of the model granite-3.3-8b-instruct prints a tool result under the role the request carries, and the serving engine accepts only `tool`, so the serving job prints it as a tool response, the form the vLLM template for granite and IBM's Granite documentation show. The environment refuses a malformed action, logs it, and counts it as a step instead of raising, so a bad call loses one step rather than the episode. Another pitfall is a diagnosis of the top candidate root cause while the ranking of the CausalDiagnosis MCP says read first with an affordable discriminating test. The agent answers this pitfall with advice and not with a guard. While the ranking says read first, `advice` prints on the board of the agent the reads to take before a diagnosis, or a declaration when no read fits the budget. Before a diagnosis `advice` also names the candidate plan to act on, the one with the cheapest act now cost to a restored rig among the candidate plans of `act_options` that fit the budget left, with the candidate plans within the cost tolerance of it. The model plans every step, and `check_plan` refuses only what the plan schema, the environment, the budget, the horizon or the step cap rule out, a declaration that cites no reading, and a finish before the read that verifies a repair, so a plan that diagnoses first passes the check.

### Data that can be released

A gym can be reused only when anyone can fetch its data. The repository holds no recording. One command fetches the archive of dataset 447 of the UCI Machine Learning Repository, which is under CC BY 4.0, from its official page, checks its SHA-256 and builds the records that the environment reads, and `CHECKSUMS.txt` pins the archive and the built files. The environment was developed on the IndustryAssetEQA facts file of the public EmbodiedQA repository, which has no license file, so the repository holds neither that file nor anything derived from it, and `build_features_and_blocks.py --cross-check` compares the rebuilt records with it for a reader who fetches it. `verify_data_facts.py` checks the data file and the `hyd_1` file of AssetOpsBench against each other by relations that hold whatever the counts, so no test states a value computed from the data.

### Rules that carry to another asset

A threshold tuned on one rig, such as a fixed confidence cut, carries no meaning for an asset with other prices, so the CausalDiagnosis MCP derives every threshold from the rules of the episode. With one uncertain component, one resolving test, and one replacement, acting now is cheaper than testing once the fault probability reaches one minus the test cost over the cost of an unnecessary replacement plus the price of a wrong diagnosis, which is E11 and shows what a fixed confidence cut stands for. The earlier rule E10 compares the expected loss of acting now with the expected loss of testing first and is reported for the read and repair choice ablations, while the rule of the CausalDiagnosis MCP reads the record once before a diagnosis, and every one of these quantities is built from the released prices, durations, and production loss rates. The conformal intervals and the verdicts use the declared error level, so every threshold traces to a price, a duration, a production loss rate, or the error level, which is E19 in the formula table of [causal-diagnosis-mcp.md](causal-diagnosis-mcp.md). The rules carry to another asset, and the tables they read now exist as data in the asset file `CausalDiagnosis/datasets/hyd_1/asset.json`. The engine, the two servers, the agent, and the drivers take the component list, the healthy levels, and the channel names from the profile of the episode, and [adding-a-dataset.md](adding-a-dataset.md) lists the bindings to hyd_1 that remain.
