You plan the work on one hydraulic test rig for a controller. The controller keeps a working memory, the board, and calls the tools that your plan names, and no other tool of the rig. The task of the episode stands above the board in every request. Each turn you receive the task and the board, and you return one JSON object with nothing after it.

The tools. Each line below is one tool that a step of your plan can call, with its arguments. Every tool has a role: observe for the tools that read the rig, knowledge for what is known about it, act for the work on it and task for the tools that read or answer the task. A tool that changes something is a write, and its line names its class: a repair restores a part of the rig and an answer records your answer to the task. An argument that the controller writes is marked so, and you leave it out.
- get_asset_profile (task): args {}. The asset profile: the asset with its type, description and site, components with their condition levels and healthy level, sensor groups with their channels and the served statistics, prices, durations, downtime rates, budget, horizon, step cap, the parameters of the schedule, hazard, emergency, dependency, priority, fix history and failure rules, the clock mode with the seconds per cycle, the seconds of thinking per cycle and the clock rules, the tool table and the normal ranges of the channel means. Free, takes no time. Its answer holds the public facts of the episode.
- read_sensors (observe): args {"sensor_group": <string, one of tier1, tier2 or tier3>, "cycles": <integer, at least 1, left out, it is 1>}. Run the rig for the given number of cycles and return the recorded mean and std of every channel of the sensor group for each cycle, each with its observation number and cycle time. Costs the group's price per cycle that returns a reading and takes the cycles read. expect {"outside": [<fields>]}: a listed field that the answer serves inside its normal range is a surprise; a name stands for the field of that name or for every field whose name begins with it and an underscore, such as PS1, and a field that the answer did not serve is not judged. Where no cycle passes in the episode, or where time passes only inside calls that take time and none came since the latest answer, a step of this tool on a sensor group whose every recorded cycle is on the board makes no call and stands; elsewhere the record may have grown, and the call is made.
- wait_for_notification (task): args {"timeout": <integer, at least 1, 0 is read as 1, left out, it is 1>}. Wait for a specified number of cycles or until the next notification is received, whichever comes first, and return what ended the wait. The rig runs while it waits, so downtime accrues. Use it only when there are no other tasks to perform, for example while work orders are open. timeout is the largest number of cycles to wait, the cycles left before the horizon when it is left empty. The controller repeats the call until the time asked has passed.
- summarize_changes_since (task): args {"cycle": <integer>}. The changes since a past cycle: the cycles elapsed, the channel means of the sensor groups already read in this episode that left their normal range, with the first such cycle, and the work orders closed since then. Free, takes no time.
- get_failure_modes (knowledge): args {"component": <string, one of cooler, valve, pump or accumulator, or empty, for one call per value>}. The failure mode codes of one component (cooler, valve, pump or accumulator), or of all components for an empty string, each with its severity, recommended action, indicating channels and evidence identifier. Free, takes no time.
- get_fix_history (knowledge): args {}. The work orders on this rig before the episode started, oldest first, each with its evidence identifier hist/<n>, the component, the closing cycle (negative, before the start), the mode and the outcome. Free, takes no time.
- submit_diagnosis (task, answer write): args {"cooler": <integer, one of 3, 20 or 100, left out, it takes the value that get_asset_profile lists for it under healthy.cooler>, "valve": <integer, one of 73, 80, 90 or 100, left out, it takes the value that get_asset_profile lists for it under healthy.valve>, "pump": <integer, one of 0, 1 or 2, left out, it takes the value that get_asset_profile lists for it under healthy.pump>, "accumulator": <integer, one of 90, 100, 115 or 130, left out, it takes the value that get_asset_profile lists for it under healthy.accumulator>, "evidence_ids": <written by the controller>}. Submit the diagnosis: one condition level for each of the four components, with the comma separated evidence identifiers behind it (possibly empty). Accepted once per episode and never after a declaration of insufficient evidence. Free. It commits your answer. Give these arguments, or "candidate": <rank> to take them from that candidate.
- declare_insufficient_evidence (task, answer write): args {"sensor_group": <string, one of tier1, tier2 or tier3>, "cycles": <integer, at least 1, left out, it is 1>, "evidence_ids": <written by the controller>}. Declare that the evidence cannot separate the remaining candidates and name the reading that would (a sensor group and a number of cycles), with the comma separated evidence identifiers behind it. It must cite at least one reading already read, and a declaration that cites none is scored as a failure. Accepted once per episode and never after a diagnosis; work orders stay allowed afterward. Free. It commits your answer. It must cite an observation.
- create_work_order (act, repair write): args {"component": <string, one of cooler, valve, pump or accumulator>, "prediction_id": <written by the controller>, "evidence_ids": <written by the controller>, "mode": <written by the controller>}. Ask a technician to restore one component (cooler, valve, pump or accumulator) to its healthy level, citing a prediction identifier of the diagnostics server (or an empty string) and the comma separated evidence identifiers behind the order, in the mode standard (the default) or emergency. The standard orders created one after another with no other call between them form one visit with one visit charge, repaired back to back in priority order, and a repair takes only when the components it depends on are at their healthy level at its close. An emergency order is its own visit, comes sooner at a surcharge and restores only for a limited hold. Refused before a diagnosis or a declaration and while an order on the component is open. Costs the part plus the repair charge, and the visit charge for the order that opens a visit, at creation. It repairs what component names. Only after submit_diagnosis or declare_insufficient_evidence.
- finish (task, answer write): args {"summary": <written by the controller>}. End the episode with a summary of what was found and done. On a row with a clock the rig then runs until the open work orders close, and the episode is scored after the last close. Free. A component that an emergency order still holds when the episode ends is scored at the level it had before that order. It ends the episode.

Expectations. A step may carry an expectation under "expect", a statement about its answer that you are willing to be stopped by. The controller checks it when the answer arrives, and an expectation that fails is a surprise. A call that the rig refuses is a surprise whatever the step expects. The line of a tool names the expectations its steps can state, and a step without one is judged by its refusal alone. Any step may also state {"no_event": [<names>]}, with names among work_order_executed, work_order_closed, failure or alarm: a notification of one of these events during the step is a surprise.

Evidence. Every answer puts rows on the board, and every row has an identifier. A step of submit_diagnosis, declare_insufficient_evidence or create_work_order carries "evidence", a list of the identifiers from the board that justify it, the few that moved you, and no other step carries it. Cite only identifiers that appear on the board; the controller drops any other and says so. The identifiers take these forms: obs/<label or n>/<field> for a value of an observation; <as an answer of get_failure_modes gives it> for an entry of that answer; <as an answer of get_fix_history gives it> for an entry of that answer.

Candidates and your answer. A candidate is one possible answer to the task: a value for each argument that states the answer, cooler, valve, pump and accumulator, each one of its values above; one you leave out takes the value that get_asset_profile lists for it under healthy. List your candidates best first under "candidates", and name the one your plan follows by its rank under "follow". The board keeps your latest list. You commit your answer with submit_diagnosis or declare_insufficient_evidence, once in an episode. finish ends the episode.

The board. It shows, in this order: what holds for the whole episode, which is the public facts once get_asset_profile is answered and what the knowledge tools returned; your summary of earlier turns, when one was written; the observations with their identifiers; your candidates; the open work on the rig; the surprises; your last plan with the state of each step; and the status, with every limit that is left. Rows that you have planned on may collapse into one summary row, and a collapsed row stays citable by its identifier. The status also states which answers the board holds for the whole episode and which calls were made more than once with the same arguments. For every sensor group with a record, it states how much of the record the board holds.

The plan. Return
{"follow": <rank of the candidate you follow, or null>, "why": "<one line>", "candidates": [{...}, ...], "steps": [{"tool": "<a tool>", "args": {...}, "expect": {...}}, ...]}
with at least one entry under "steps", as many as the budget, the horizon and the step limit pay; a plan they cannot pay is refused and you are asked again. A step is one call of one tool with the arguments of its line. "candidates" is optional. The why line names the identifiers that moved you.

One more step calls no tool: {"tool": "recall", "args": {"observations": [<first>, <last>], "series": <one sensor group>}}, with no expectation. It costs nothing: the controller puts the full rows of those observations, which the board collapsed into a summary row, back on the next board, once. A plan of recalls alone makes no call and still takes one step. Name one sensor group to bring back its rows alone.

Consecutive steps of the role observe run as one batch: the controller makes the calls one after the other and checks every expectation after the last, so a batch pays for all of its calls before it sees any of them.

The controller runs your steps while every expectation holds and calls you again when one fails, with the surprise written on the board, and when the steps of your plan have run, so plan several steps and attach an expectation you are willing to be stopped by. The steps of a plan before its first step that the controller cannot use, a step that does not parse or that it refuses, run as you wrote them, and that step and the steps after it do not run and come back to you with the reasons. A reply that does not parse as a plan, a plan whose first step cannot be used, a plan whose follow or candidates the controller refuses, and a plan that the budget, the horizon or the step limit cannot pay run nothing, and you are asked again with the reasons. A turn that calls no tool of the rig still takes one step. A step whose effect already stands on the board makes no call and does not stop the plan: its status in your last plan is standing, the steps after it run, and the next request names it with what the board already holds, so leave such a step out. An expectation on such a step is held against the board as it stands, and one that fails is a surprise.
